## Purpose, classification and authoritative boundaries

Manufacturing owns **the planning, authorised execution and history of transforming or repairing goods**, from feasible production demand through work-order completion, including production instructions, version-pinned executable material/operation plans, resource and capacity scheduling, operation progress, rework execution, production movement demand and production-specific performance/cost evidence. It supports small, manually managed workshops and complex multi-step, batch, discrete, composite, outsourced and multi-site production without making any specialised machinery, solver, external provider or GUI mandatory.

**Owner-approved 21 November 2026:** Manufacturing owns production plans, executable manufacturing BOMs (mBOMs), routings, released work orders, shop-floor operations, production/rework choices and operational yield; **Inventory** alone owns physical quantity, stock reservations/issue/returns, WIP stock positions, physical lot/serial genealogy, scrap quantities, custodial state and committed finished-stock receipts. Manufacturing requests Inventory stock transitions, distinguishing *requested, pending, refused, unknown and committed*; even a completed work order does not itself alter stock. Inventory's RINV-01–14 remain approved and INV-02's production-owner ambiguity is resolved. Simple stock-only kit conversions without production routings, work instructions, labour/machine or quality execution may remain Inventory-native.

**Product** defines raw materials, consumables, intermediate and finished products, their approved design specifications/engineering BOMs, units and revisions regardless of whether saleable. Manufacturing derives and authorises a **version-pinned executable mBOM and routing**, including stage-specific materials, allowances, substitutions and resources, without rewriting Product's design history. **Accounting** alone owns recognised costs, fiscal valuation and journals. **Warehouse** owns stores, picking, staging, handling execution and put-away; **Logistics** owns external transport evidence; **Asset** owns managed equipment/building identities, condition and maintenance; **Facility** is the owner-approved candidate for operational premises layouts, site circulation and qualified zone restrictions; **Location** provides spatial references without certifying route safety.

**Quality — approved reciprocal split (MFG-24; QTY-01–12):** Manufacturing retains **native production checks and operational quality gates** without a separately selected Quality service. When an independent Quality gate is configured or required, Quality alone owns its method, observations, assessment, non-conformance and **final technical result/sign-off**; Manufacturing consumes that result and retains the separate **production continuation, hold or rework instruction**, without a competing technical verdict. Inventory alone commits physical stock restrictions/releases. Missing or uncertain mandatory Quality results block affected production work. Quality owns ISO 9001 governance. Clinical assessments remain with clinical owners. Callable interfaces remain to be published.

**Owner-approved Quality public-interface clarification (22 November 2026; Q1–Q5):** [Quality](../quality/capability-record.md) owns independent technical inspection verdicts only for gates expressly delegated to it by approved effective policy; Manufacturing keeps work execution, its operational gate and native inspection where Quality is absent. Manufacturing sends scoped work-order, gate and operation revisions and receives versioned pass/fail/indeterminate evidence. Neither a Quality pass nor an outbox event commits Manufacturing's work release. Missing, revoked or contradictory required Quality evidence prevents a claimed successful operational gate. Host-composed public interfaces avoid a hard cyclic package import; original owner-approved boundary is unchanged.

Manufacturing does **not** own source technical test certificates, customer relationship masters, external party identities, operator credentials, AuthN/AuthZ grants, specialist legal standards, asset maintenance, facility safety approval, payment, purchasing commitments, invoice publication, transport custody or any neighbour's private tables. One credible owner per source fact; use published ports/events rather than circular or private imports.

## Owned records, lifecycle and correctness

| Manufacturing-owned record | Approved conceptual content and lifecycle |
|---|---|
| Production definition / executable mBOM | Product source revision, quantities and UoM, approved alternates, recipes/lay-up or process parameters where applicable, operation route, resources and quality gates; draft → checked → released → superseded/retired; historical work orders retain pins |
| Demand and finite-feasibility plan | Demand source, priority, due date, required stages, stock feasibility, resource availability, proposed capacity and revisioned replan; estimates and limitations visible |
| Work order and subject reference | Tenant/site/customer-confidential scope, type, subject/target Product and item reference, source condition or planned output, pinned instructions, approval, assignments and deadlines; approved common lifecycle below |
| Operation execution | Work centre, operator/resource evidence, start/pause/complete, partial quantity, downtime, observations, discrepancies, verified material-handling/Quality references and corrections |
| Production movement plan | Origin/destination, facility-network revision, handling requirements, suggested route, transport/work request, estimated time and separate execution/Inventory-confirmation states |
| Production disposition / rework | Response to Quality decisions, repair instructions, authorised scope, held/waiting/rework/completed reason and evidence; never source inspection findings |
| Production cost/yield evidence | Measured time, material use acknowledgements, losses, subcontract reference, estimated versus confirmed drivers; not booked cost or recognised WIP |

**Approved conceptual work-order states (MFG-21):** draft → checked/approved → released → in progress/part-complete ↔ paused/held/waiting → awaiting independent quality/stock confirmation as required → completed → closed. Cancelled, disputed and superseded are explicit governed branches; corrective events retain the original factual history. These states describe approved lifecycle meaning, not implemented or frozen wire-enum values.

**New manufacture:** produce target product quantities; **repair/refurbishment:** reference the *existing customer-owned or otherwise externally owned item*, its condition/identity/custody, agreed assessment and approved work scope; **rework:** reference original work order and Quality disposition; **urgent crash repair:** change authorised priority without bypassing restricted steps. Where no stock product applies, a service/repair item reference and its authoritative source must be supported rather than inventing manufactured inventory. Composite manufacture is modelled by versioned process/recipe, batch, storage, lay-up/curing and technical examination evidence **where applicable**—these are fictional examples, not claims about any named business's practices.

**Non-negotiable correctness:** tenant/resource authorisation on every protected action; no work release using an invalid/expired/pending critical revision; don't fabricate stock, custody, personnel qualifications, quality approval or machine safety from a plan or scan; no double operation/material commits on retry; explicit units, revisions, effective/recorded times, idempotency, provenance and correction chains. Unknown downstream completion stays unknown until reconciled. Source owner events are committed via its durable outbox, carried at least once through transport; do not promise a global exactly-once transaction.

**Planned public commands (approved responsibility; signatures not published):** define/revise/release production definition; assess plan and shortages; create/check/approve/release/replan/suspend/cancel work order; assign/start/pause/complete stage; request/observe material handling; record rework or manufacturing disposition; accept external owner acknowledgements; reconcile disputes/corrections. Commands carry scoped actor and decision context, revisions and idempotency; return committed, refused, pending or unknown outcome as appropriate.

**Planned public queries (approved responsibility; signatures not published):** versioned instructions and mBOM/routing; work order and stage; resource loading; material demand; planned vs last-confirmed position; **Item Journey** as a strictly permission-checked composition from Manufacturing, Inventory, Warehouse, Quality and Facility, including source freshness/unknowns; reported measures with provenance.

**Illustrative events:** `ProductionDefinitionReleased`, `WorkOrderReleased`, `OperationProgressCommitted`, `ProductionDispositionCommitted`, `MaterialTransferRequested`, `ProductionCorrectionCommitted`, `WorkOrderClosed`. Names are conceptual, not a wire schema; source-owned durable outbox and replay/idempotency apply. A Manufacturing event cannot assert a completed Quality examination or Inventory movement.

## Manufacturing strategies and movement planning

**Mapping/routing architecture approved 21 November 2026:** Facility alone owns versioned approved physical site maps, internal network restrictions and approved travel graph (Location supplies geometry; Asset owns the managed building); Manufacturing owns work-centre production routing, material movement demand, route selection and dispatch decision; Warehouse/handling executes movements, Inventory confirms stock/custody. A reusable framework-independent **stateless graph/routing library** performs shortest-path, constrained route, optional multi-stop and solver calculations on supplied approved graph + constraints, but owns neither map, movement plan nor operational state. Facility may use it for connectivity checking; Logistics may use the same computational library with its own transport network. The host composes Facility's graphical map editor and Manufacturing's plant-flow overlay without duplicate source records. Dynamic closure invalidates affected route suggestions; a routing result never certifies human/vehicle safety or controls industrial robots. Exact algorithm/library packaging and data exchange schema remain an implementation choice.

Native minimum: single-step or staged work order, manual planning, approved resource assignments, material request and confirmed acknowledgement, partial execution, correction/rework, outsourced processing references and meaningful item tracking. Support make-to-order/stock, configured batch/discrete and engineer-to-order modes where needed. Multi-constraint APS solvers and continuous-process optimisation require later demonstrated value.

**Owner-approved MFG-FLOW-01–10:** Plant Layout & Material Flow Planning, scaled and revisioned 2D editor, connected travel network, source-confirmed physical stock, Manufacturing-controlled production movement planning, reuse of shared stateless routing algorithms where justified, scan/QR-first tracking, optional simulation and robotics/sensors, unified accessible shop-floor UI and safe/authorised movement confirmation. **Facility** owns qualified site-wide operational layouts, doors, zones, directional/vehicle restrictions and closures; **Manufacturing** uses approved graph revision to propose internal work-centre routes and transfers, not to certify physical clearance or issue safety-critical robot commands. **Warehouse** performs handling and **Inventory** confirms physical movement; route proposal, operator scan and provider acceptance are not transfer completion. An imported PDF, SVG or DXF is drawing evidence, not an approved traversable network. Invalidated/closed edges require safe stop/replan. A shared stateless solver can implement graph/vehicle algorithms without owning domain state. Offline capture stays pending until validated and reconciled.

**Scheduling:** propose feasible work from actual qualified capacity, materials, known outages and due times; authorised emergency work may preempt planned tasks with recorded affected jobs, customer obligations, notification and supervisor approval where required; quality, safety, confidentiality and stock invariants are unchanged. Queue/waiting times and simulation outputs are estimates with recorded assumptions. **MFG-25 is approved**; particular solver algorithms and port signatures remain implementation choices.

**Outsourcing:** retain manufacturing operation intent and supplier processing proof, with independently authoritative source commercial commitments, Inventory in-transit/contractor custody and Logistics transport evidence. A supplier's “complete” message never creates received or quality-released stock. Costs remain evidence until Accounting decides recognition.

## Reciprocal Logging, Audit and Reporting interfaces — approved conceptual ownership

**Logging (technical diagnostics):** Manufacturing **inputs** are a trusted, policy-restricted logging intake, optional scoped diagnostics query and sink/health status (accepted, rejected, throttled, unavailable or uncertain). Manufacturing **outputs** are safely minimised structured technical failures, timing and retry/provider/scan/solver exception codes with permitted opaque correlation. Logging does not decide business state, preserve Audit evidence or certify production completion. Avoid secrets, customer designs, medical/employee data and raw method measurements in diagnostics.

**Audit (accountable evidence):** Manufacturing **inputs** are producer-authorised Audit capture policy, accepted/rejected/pending/unknown receipt and limited integrity/coverage/review references. Manufacturing **outputs** are risk-selected source-owned committed decisions (release/cancel/close, amendments, exceptional reprioritisation, rework/production disposition, restricted access/override and correction) via its durable outbox with event ID, source version, actor/assurance, effective/observed times and resource scope; use minimised references, not entire business payloads. Audit independently verifies custody and review, not source truth. Inventory emits movement facts, Quality emits technical outcomes, Authorisation emits permission decisions, each to Audit under their own contracts.

**Reporting (derived decision support):** Manufacturing **inputs** are authorised report/analytics definition, execution and refresh status, report reference, scope and freshness/completeness. Manufacturing **outputs** are versioned, authorised work-order/operation, planned vs actual progress, load, capacity, hours, downtime, yield, rework, waste and cost **driver** facts, corrections and source event/revision for projection refresh. Reporting owns KPIs, aggregates, snapshots, visualisations and exports; no source master or production decision is replaced. For multi-owner OEE, cost and yield reports, Reporting queries Inventory, Quality, Asset and Accounting through their public ports with explicit missing/unknown and scope; do not infer facts from Manufacturing's provisional estimates. Reporting outage cannot block native production execution.

**Shared integration rule:** each capability enforces server-side tenant, customer/project and resource-level access; none accesses Manufacturing private tables or treats event delivery as proof of the other owner's completed operation. Correlate diagnostics, audit evidence and reports using permitted opaque identifiers and version/time provenance; protected reports and diagnostic queries must not leak confidential client activity.

## Security, privacy, policy and resilience

Every owner enforces tenant, group, role, project/work-order and individual record access. Customer identity and protected work need not be visible to the whole shop floor: use minimally revealing job labels, narrow station instructions, authorised evidential references, safe notifications and no cross-tenant search/heatmap leaks. Customer and supplier identities may exist without a platform account; account discovery/claiming, duplicate-principal reconciliation and verification belong to the separately approved **QTY-01, ID-MATCH-01–04 and ID-CLAIM-01–05** cross-capability review, not Manufacturing. A completed account claim never unions Manufacturing access rights. Operator/device assurance, retention, legal holds, disputed ownership, personal-information minimisation and qualified safety requirements require source and decision provenance.

Plan and execution may remain available in reduced/manual mode when optional providers fail. Protected production release requiring unknown material/quality/qualification/site safety is blocked or held, with explicit reason and retry path. A network outage or offline scan must not invent stock movements, outcomes or proof. Durable owner outboxes, correlation, idempotency, reconciliation and audit evidence support recovery.

**Presentation rules:**
1. **Progressive disclosure:** a basic workshop may expose only Dashboard, Work Orders and Shop Floor. Complex plants may reveal Planning, Definitions, Item Journey, Material Flow, Resources and detailed production evidence. Capabilities absent from a host must be hidden, disabled or shown with honest unavailable status, never fake data.
2. **One composite operator workspace:** Material handling calls Warehouse/Inventory, quality examination uses Quality, map editing uses Facility, reports use Reporting; Manufacturing orchestrates the *presentation* of public results without owning those records. Facility map edits and site safety approval are separate from Manufacturing's material movement planning.
3. **Safety and correctness:** distinguish estimated, requested, queued, offline pending, held, rejected, unknown and source-confirmed statuses. Confirmations appear only after the responsible source commits; hold unsafe actions when essential qualification, stock, Quality or Facility evidence is missing. A diagram or optimiser never certifies route safety.
4. **Authorisation:** route/sidebar visibility is convenience, not access control. All screen data and mutations use server-side Authorisation and source-domain permissions, with per-tenant/site/customer/project and work-order scope, protected evidence disclosure and protected approval/step-up as applicable.
5. **Mobile usability:** responsive compact operator cards, accessible large targets, scan-first with manual ID fallback, guarded shared devices, Android-compatible PWA/thin client option, explicit offline pending and reconciliation without duplicate actions; preserve Inventory RINV-13/14.
6. **Accessibility and branding:** Theme Manager public semantic tokens, no hard-coded competing colour mode; keyboard and screen reader traversal, accessible table/list alternative to material-flow graph, WCAG 2.2 AA, responsive layouts, reduced motion, i18n and locale-aware dates/quantities.
7. **Host composition:** a derived `application-test-template` app can add the navigation entry, public-route adapters, local fixtures and acceptance scenarios. The base test template's `test` mode is unauthenticated and **must not be deployed publicly**. Production host composes proper IAM/session and authorization providers behind a supported public host contract; don't use Theme Manager's internal unpublished integration hooks.

**GUI conceptual acceptance:** clicking Manufacturing opens its dashboard without replacing the shared sidebar or top bar; roles see authorised tiles/routes only; Work Order → Shop Floor → Item Journey preserves its item and tenant context; pending stock/scanner event never appears confirmed; restricted operator cannot see customer secrets, Quality reports, other-tenants' items or supervisor controls; Facility restriction change invalidates an affected displayed route; absent optional Reporting/Facility/Quality yields accurate reduced-mode indicators. UI directives are advisory; exact route names, component names, screen consolidation and navigation grouping are host design choices, subject to public contract and usability validation.

**Design rule:** classify standards as (a) common architectural/reference baseline, (b) conditional process or regulated controls, or (c) optional interoperability profiles. A recommendation to align an interface with a standard is **not** a claim of certification, legal compliance or mandated adoption. Use effective-dated edition, jurisdiction, contractual applicability, profile owner and traceable applicability decision; authoritative specialist controls belong to relevant source owners and do not create a new capability automatically. Check current edition before implementing.

## Public contract summary and dependency requirements

**Evidence:** No published Manufacturing implementation-root `CONTRACT.md` has been verified. The inbound/outbound tables and commands, queries and event descriptions above are the **planned** public contract; exact signatures, transport and independently verified implementation remain future work.

**Five input families:** production definitions; demand/orders; stage execution; qualified acknowledgements from other source owners; planning and authorised queries. **Five output families:** work-order and routing facts; requests to stock/handling/Quality owners; production disposition and trace; qualified cost/completion/exception evidence; committed events and history. An event or request cannot attest to another source owner's completion.

**Necessary for the relevant protected operation:** trusted Identity/Authentication/Authorisation context, approved Product facts where applicable, authoritative Inventory acknowledgements for physical stock, and qualified Quality, Facility safety, workforce and legal/policy requirements **when mandatory for that operation**. Quality-absent hosts retain native Manufacturing checks, not independent Quality sign-off. Other capability integrations, CAD, robotics, solver, Mapping GUI and Reporting are conditional or optional. Missing essential evidence blocks only the affected unsafe transition; optional absence yields honest reduced functionality.

**Conceptual completeness:** Core design is sufficiently covered **subject to final cross-branch reconciliation and explicit owner sign-off**. This does **not** establish implementation, independent root contract, certification, tests, operational readiness or mergeability.
