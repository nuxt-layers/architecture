## Status, purpose and classification

**Established governing distinction:** [Identity and Access](https://github.com/nuxt-layers/architecture-rules/blob/master/docs/identity.md) gives Identity exclusive authority over identities, tenants, groups and direct group-membership lifecycle. [Capability Standard](https://github.com/nuxt-layers/architecture-rules/blob/master/docs/capability-standard.md) requires cohesive independently justified responsibilities, owned state/outbox and public acyclic contracts. The governing [Catalogue](https://github.com/nuxt-layers/architecture-rules/blob/master/docs/catalogue.md) lists paid/scheme `membership` as a *candidate*, not an implemented capability. Applicable accepted ADRs and normative security/privacy/presentation standards prevail over this working record.

**Agreed responsibility (MEM-01–MEM-02):** Membership operates voluntary or governed **participation in a named scheme**—for example a professional association, sports club programme, charitable supporter scheme or loyalty/benefits membership. It evaluates scheme-specific admission, terms acceptance, classifications, tenure, renewals, suspensions, termination and **benefit eligibility** with attributable effective times. This has an independent business lifecycle even when no purchase, app account, recurring payment or Identity group join occurs. It is therefore a defensible **Domain** boundary, but not automatically an independent package or Nuxt Layer.

**Primary consumers:** scheme administrators, scheme participants (including people without an Identity account), authorised verifying services, Booking, Events, Pricing, Order, Subscription, Billing and Reporting. Scheme participation is **not** application authorisation or tenancy ownership.

**Evidence status:** Accepted governing rules take precedence. **Owner-approved** means the agreed conceptual Membership design, not a released contract. **Illustrative** denotes proposed routes, states or examples rather than a fixed API. **Unverified** denotes missing implementation or compliance evidence.

## Authoritative records and strict exclusions

| Membership owns — agreed conceptual responsibility | Remains authoritative elsewhere |
|---|---|
| Versioned scheme definition, scheme category/level, eligibility criteria and administrative effective dates | Identity's groups, hierarchy, principal status and **group-membership associations** |
| Participation application, evidence references, admission/decline and controlled appeal/review state | Verification's source attestations; Profile's personal attributes; Legal Compliance's statutory determination |
| Scheme-specific participant reference and approved binding to a business party or consented Identity link | Business-party/customer identity and contact details, Identity account, Authentication sessions |
| Accepted scheme terms/version, consent *reference* where relevant and participation period | Privacy's consent record/legal-basis rules, Publisher's terms text, legally binding contract determinations |
| Membership participation lifecycle, scheme renewal offers and participation state | Subscription's commercial plan subscription lifecycle and platform/seat feature entitlements |
| Benefit eligibility **under scheme rules**, use limits and benefit consumption ledger where indispensable | Pricing's tariffs/discount calculation; Booking's inventory/capacity; Order's sale; Billing's charges; Payment Gateway's money movement |
| Pro-rata membership *eligibility/period* consequences, not calculated charges | Billing's monetary proration, charge cycles and disputes; Invoice's legal documents; Accounting's ledger |
| Membership authoritative decisions, revisions and domain outbox | Audit's evidence custody, Logging's diagnostics, Message Queue's delivery, Reporting's aggregates |

**Key invariant:** Participation, an Identity group relationship, a Subscription entitlement, a payment receipt and a permission decision are **different facts**. No implicit conversion between them. A scheme may intentionally require an active Identity-group relationship, but Identity must independently execute its authorised lifecycle; Membership only requests or receives a result through a public contract. A failed or absent group change does not masquerade as success. **Accepted ADR-0017:** installation, tenant, Identity group, real-world legal entity and scheme are distinct scopes; tenant or group parentage, creator, payer or legal-entity status grants no implicit scheme administration, beneficiary access or cross-tenant rights. Active tenant governance remains Identity-owned and cannot be inferred from membership.

## Native service and lifecycle

**Scheme setup:** authorised administrators define independently evidenced organisational and tenant scope (not inferred from a group hierarchy), effective term rules, levels and eligibility, admission/review criteria, benefits, quotas, grace periods, renewal windows, cessation, data retention and published terms version. Changes are versioned; existing participants preserve agreed effective-policy history.

**Application and admission:** create a participant application from an authorised party reference or privacy-safe guest identifier; supply declared evidence/terms acknowledgement; check required attestations and capacity/eligibility constraints, require human decision when policy demands; accept, waitlist, decline or withdraw with reason and review rights. Do not silently make an external customer an Identity account.

**Participation states:** `applied → pending_review → approved_pending_effective → active → grace | suspended → active | ended | expired`; `applied/pending_review → rejected | withdrawn`; approved pending effective may return to review, be cancelled, or expire. **These are conceptual states**; any automatic transition needs an explicit configured policy, effective time, reason and evidence. Historical facts remain stable, corrections append superseding decisions.

**Renewal:** create an offer/renewal intent and retain consent or approval; distinguish requested renewal, legally accepted commitment, charge requested, invoice issued, payment settled and **new membership term effective**. Policy determines whether verified payment is a precondition; uncertain settlement means `pending` rather than automatic access. Grace, cancellation, cooling-off, backdating and refund consequences are explicit, never inferred.

**Benefit decisions:** answer scheme-based eligibility for a concrete benefit/action and effective time, with scope, level, available quota, qualifying membership version, policy provenance, reservation/consumption result and validity window. A positive membership-benefit result is **not** permission to access a resource, a final price, a booked seat, or evidence of physical fulfilment. Quota consumption must be idempotent and compensated/reconciled when Booking/Order fails.

**Recovery:** reject stale policy/version and mismatched beneficiary/tenant; retain recoverable pending external evidence and an operator review queue; on replays return the prior result; use source-owned transactional outbox for committed events, delayed/reordered delivery tolerance, reconciliation and explicit remediation. No exactly-once cross-system guarantee.

## Configuration, security, privacy and provider modes

Membership owns the interpretation and enforcement of its scheme rules. Under **accepted ADR-0018**, distinguish mandatory architecture/security invariants, bounded platform configuration, verified organisational rules and governing instruments (for example a constitution), qualified legal/Privacy restrictions, business rules and separately published terms. Each has a recognised authority, effective version, jurisdiction/scope, approval, change/withdrawal and unknown-state semantics. Configuration registers approved settings, but neither creates legal authority nor overrides hard floors; Publisher publishes terms without certifying enforcement. Scheme settings are validated and versioned: eligibility/levels, dues, admission, benefits/quotas, renewal/grace, jurisdiction, notices, retention and provider authority. Protected changes require independent approval/assurance as appropriate and cannot be immediately weakened and exploited by the same actor. Rights and fees are never derived from untrusted client flags.

Enforce server-side ASVS 5.0 Level 2 baseline, stronger assurance for privileged admission overrides, financial-impact changes, unusual benefit issuance, identity linkage and mass exports. Deny cross-tenant and cross-scheme leakage, prevent ID enumeration, duplicate enrolment, benefit replay, forged payment webhooks, stale attestation and administrator self-dealing. Support separation of duties, review trails, anonymisation where law permits, protected correction, held records, erasure requests, least-purpose disclosure and data minimisation. WCAG 2.2 AA and locale-safe formatting apply to UI.

**External participants and account claiming:** As reinforced by the owner-approved generalised domains/party-relationships review (former review: party relationships and generalised domains 2026 11 21), maintain scheme-specific, effective-dated participant/beneficiary/payer relationships without creating a generic Party/Customer package or forcing Identity accounts. Later account linking requires separate verified claimant evidence and scheme-specific permission; matching email/name/provider ID or a tenant relationship does not establish identity or access. Never automatically merge principal memberships or expose unrelated scheme records.

**Provider neutrality:** `native`, `external-authoritative` and carefully scoped `hybrid` are potential per-scheme modes; each must declare exactly who decides admission, renewal and status, trusted source revision, synchronisation latency, suspended/unknown states, conflict handling, import/export, migration, withdrawal and cost approval. Integrations owns transport/credentials, not programme decisions. Unavailable external authority blocks positive external-only membership/benefit assertions; imported replicas are marked derived and stale.

No jurisdiction-specific claim about UK consumer cancellation, charity/association obligations, regulated profession membership, VAT or cooling-off is made without qualified legal validation and effective-date evidence.

**Contract flows:** A protected command is first authorised; Membership validates and applies or refuses the owned business operation. An expected refusal returns a **normalised safe error** to the caller; serious technical problems additionally emit **minimised Logging diagnostics**. High-risk *attempts and committed decisions* are classified for **Audit** according to the approved risk/event catalogue; committed changes go from Membership's own transaction/outbox to authorised Audit and downstream consumers, with deduplication and provenance. **Reporting** reads authorised, freshness-qualified Membership facts and may consume lifecycle events to refresh derived projections. Shared identifiers and timestamps permit correlation, but no output to Logging, Audit or Reporting becomes a second Membership source of truth.

**Separation examples:** a denied application gives a safe business error and attributable approval/denial evidence when policy requires; a provider timeout yields `pending/unknown` rather than a fabricated rejection or payment; duplicate benefit redemption returns the original idempotent result while any attempted abuse may be audited by risk classification; report generation may lag while Membership remains authoritative; failure to deliver an audit message is tracked as a source-outbox/coverage gap rather than turning a technical log into evidence.

**Acceptance:** validate cross-tenant errors cannot leak existence; friendly localised error messages are driven by `messageKey`, not raw failures; business-denial/audit distinction; audit replay/quarantine and gap detection; log outage independence; reporting refresh after correction/revocation; restricted small-cohort exports; consistent source IDs across all four consumers. No universal mandated payload shape beyond accepted governing shared contract shapes.

## Approved cross-cutting design safeguards (22 November 2026)

These safeguards are **owner-approved parts of MEM-01–MEM-10**, not additional decision IDs or authorisation to implement.

1. **Eligibility and evidence:** Check age, qualifications, professional standing and other required conditions through the responsible qualified source. Preserve source, subject binding, expiry, revocation, effective-time and uncertainty. Unverified self-claims never count as verified evidence; unavailable compulsory evidence holds admission or the particular benefit.
2. **Privacy and security:** Enforce server-side tenant/scheme isolation, least privilege, minimum personal-data collection and disclosure, governed retention and legal holds. Apply stronger Authentication assurance and independent approval where risk warrants; treat consent, authorisation and legal basis as distinct.
3. **Cancellation and refunds:** Separate the authoritative cessation/effective-date decision (Membership) from commercial charge adjustments (Billing), refunds/settlement (Payment Gateway), issued credit documents (Invoice) and ledger entries (Accounting). Apply qualified jurisdiction-specific terms, withdrawal rights and legal holds; never imply a refund was executed from a cancellation request.
4. **Recovery and consistency:** Deduplicate operations and events; detect stale/reordered messages, payment reversal, quota drift and provider outage. Preserve pending/unknown/failed outcomes, source revisions, traceable compensation and authorised reconciliation. Do not declare cross-system operations successful before every required owner confirms its own action.
5. **Configuration constraints:** Apply validated, versioned scheme-level rule choices with effective dates, tenancy/organiser scoping and change approval. Statutory requirements, privacy/security floors and authoritative provider restrictions override local configuration and preferences; invalid or contradictory settings fail closed.

**Acceptance implications:** Include evidence revoked mid-term, lawful guest rights requests, cancellation with a failed refund, duplicate settlement or benefit-redemption replay, external provider outage, policy changes during renewal and attempted administrator override of a mandatory restriction. These are conceptual criteria, not executed tests.

### Dashboard layout and interaction

One capability dashboard should show (1) scheme/context selector and permitted actions; (2) a small set of clearly qualified status summaries (active, pending review, expiring, exceptions); (3) review/renewal/exception work queues with urgency and next action; (4) links to schemes, participants, benefits and settings. Each metric needs its own effective time, source owner, completeness and freshness where applicable, not an unlabeled all-tenant aggregate. Search, filters, pagination, empty states and clearly distinguished pending/unknown/outage states support large schemes. Preserve the scheme and filter context when navigating; use confirmation and recorded reason for high-risk irreversible actions.

**Personas/permissions:** participant sees only their lawful data/benefits and their own renewal/cancellation; scheme administrator controls assigned schemes within authorised tenant/organisation; reviewer has narrowly scoped approval/rejection duties (with separation of duties where required); privileged operator sees bounded reconciliation; auditor sees only authorised evidence references. A beneficiary, payer, delegated administrator and Identity group member are **not inherently the same actor**; access must be decided separately. Show disabled/unavailable actions with a useful explanation, but enforce **every action and query on the server**, rechecking assurance and revocation.

**GUI-facing contract:** expose authorised list/detail/summary queries, validated command outcomes with `pending` versus `completed`, stable safe error codes/`messageKey` through Error Normalisation, explicit permission/availability/read-model provenance, version/revision conflicts and event-driven refresh hints. Screens consume these public contracts and never access private tables, raw payment provider SDKs, verification stores or financial data. Host owns route integration; Membership keeps durable scheme decisions. No automatic sidebar registration, auto-derived permission or shared application database is assumed.

**Accessibility and internationalisation:** apply Theme Manager's public semantic token vocabulary (not custom competing themes), WCAG 2.2 AA for keyboard navigation, focus, forms, tables, confirmations, error summaries and responsive sidebar; screen-reader announced result and validation states; reduced motion and high contrast; BCP 47 locale/CLDR formatting and Clock-supplied effective dates/time zones. Do not rely on colour alone to distinguish active, suspended, expired, payment-unknown or stale provider state. Public/guest forms require anti-abuse protections and careful privacy/consent messages.

**Conceptual GUI acceptance:** sidebar link opens `/membership` using a derived test host without changing shared shell; routes adapt to correct permissions and guest/self-service roles; mobile collapsed sidebar remains operable; unauthorised caller denied server-side even by direct URL; review decision updates Membership and Audit evidence correctly; renewal awaiting settlement remains pending; declined/suspended/expired statuses are distinct; optional Billing or provider absence hides irrelevant screens without breaking free/native flows; scheme selection never leaks between tenants; safe localised error; accessibility and Theme Manager visual states; no duplicate business state in the UI. Acceptance will be performed by CI/user when implementation exists; none executed in this conceptual review.

## Architectural challenge, decisions and follow-up

- **MEM-01 — Classification (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** retain an independent *scheme participation* Domain boundary, rather than collapse it into Identity or Subscription. Its own admission, terms, benefits and renewal state justify it; package and deployment decisions deferred.
- **MEM-02 — Scheme authority (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** Membership owns programme participation and benefit qualification; Identity always owns group membership, Subscription commercial subscription rights and Authorisation access decisions.
- **MEM-03 — Participants (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** allow non-Identity guests through verified, privacy-minimised party references; optional consensual Identity linkage never rewrites Identity/Profile.
- **MEM-04 — Commercial coupling (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** free/manual scheme native mode; paid membership coordinates commercial owners' confirmed facts, with no fictitious charge, payment, invoice or order.
- **MEM-05 — Benefit consumption (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** scheme-qualified benefits plus idempotent quotas, while Pricing/Booking/Events independently own their own outcomes and compensation.
- **MEM-06 — Renewal/cessation (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** explicitly separate enrolment, billing, settlement, effective term, grace, pause, cancellation, expiry and legally governed refunds.
- **MEM-07 — External schemes (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** provider-neutral native/external/hybrid authority with versioned reconciliation and fail-closed uncertainty.
- **MEM-08 — Contract and GUI (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** public authorised commands/queries/outbox events and optional WCAG-compliant GUI, no private dependencies.
- **MEM-09 — Multiple beneficiaries and delegated administration (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** support family, corporate and team arrangements using explicit versioned scheme-specific participant, beneficiary, payer and legal-party references. A payer need not be a beneficiary, and eligibility, liability and communication permissions are not inferred from one another. Authorised delegation/transfer requires policy checks, consent where applicable, effective-time history, scope and revocation. Never infer Identity group membership, resource access or hierarchy inheritance.
- **MEM-10 — Payment-conditioned activation (owner-approved 22 November 2026):** activation requirements are configured per scheme and jurisdiction subject to qualified legal duties. Free or manually administered schemes may activate on verified approval; paid schemes may require independently confirmed settlement or an explicitly authorised credit/waiver policy. Keep accepted commercial obligations, invoicing, attempted payment, settled payment, refund and effective membership state distinct. Pending/uncertain payment never satisfies a settlement precondition; reversal, grace and cancellation follow effective versioned policy.

**Technical and legal verification remaining:** confirm actual cooperating public contracts, trustworthy participant binding, external authority reconciliation and service limits, privacy-preserving duplicate checks, benefit counters, historical effective-time semantics, qualified jurisdiction-specific cancellation/tax obligations, and approved provider editions. Resolve by source/contract evidence and legal specialists, not invented platform claims.

**Conceptual completion and implementation acceptance:** The owner-approved conceptual specification is complete for the reviewed scope: independent purpose, ownership and exclusions; state/lifecycle; inbound and outbound contracts; cross-capability and reciprocal boundaries; failure modes; provider rules; security, privacy and law; standards and libraries; optional GUI; and acceptance examples. **Deferred implementation work:** publish independent root `CONTRACT.md` if a package is authorised; architecture-specific API/version model; host composition, stores/outbox, idempotency, provider adapter, threat model, storage register, consumer/UI acceptance and CI tests. This conceptual review does **not** establish implementation readiness or compliance certification. **No unresolved owner-level conceptual choice is known within MEM-01–MEM-10 or the approved GUI/library direction.** Implementation, legal applicability, current-master reconciliation and CI/acceptance remain independent requirements. Superseded review text remains in Git history instead of repeated appendices.
